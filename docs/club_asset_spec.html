How the pieces fit together

One rule drives the whole pack: geometry is shared, texture is the story. A club's rarity, price, and history are told entirely through its skin.

  • Five head meshes total — driver, fairway wood, hybrid, iron, wedge — each built once. Every model of that type, from a $8 grocery-store wedge to a $170 tour grind, sits on the exact same head, shaft, and grip geometry. Only the material changes.
  • One shaft+grip rig shared by all five non-putter heads: straight steel shaft, standard-taper black grip. Length/lie can be a uniform scale on the shaft bone if you rig it, but static geometry is fine for a bag/inspection view.
  • Iron sets and wedges are catalog singles, not six separate meshes. The game treats "4-9 irons" as one inventory item, and each wedge (PW/GW/SW/LW) as its own item on the shared wedge mesh — so one iron-head mesh and one wedge-head mesh cover the whole set.
  • Putters break the rule on purpose. All nine putter-slot items — the clubhouse loaner, the seven collectible putters, and the admin-only Auto-Putt — get their own original head geometry. This is the one place to actually design, not reskin.
Target budget: this game runs on the Quaternius stylized low-poly nature kit, so club heads should stay in that visual register — clean stylized forms, no photoreal micro-detail. Aim for 600–1,200 tris per non-putter head (whole club incl. shaft/grip under ~1,800), 1,000–2,000 tris for putter heads, one 1024×1024 albedo/ORM texture set per model (a 512 atlas is fine for the loaners), baked normals from a mid-poly sculpt if you want the milling/forging detail to read up close.

The condition system (applies to every club)

Every instance carries a condition float from 0–1, independent of rarity or price — a common club can be mint, a legendary one can be trashed. Build wear as a second, condition-driven layer on top of the base skin: a grime/scuff mask blended in by condition, plus paint-chip and rust decals placed at fixed UV spots so they can be masked in and out.

01

The five shared club-head meshes

Build each of these once, fully UV'd for a tileable-then-masked material. Every reskin below just swaps the material on this same mesh.

Wood family · 01

Driver head

Large hollow shell, 460cc-class silhouette: broad shallow face, rounded high-toe crown, flat-ish sole with a slight bulge, short bent-neck hosel. Modern oversized-wood proportions — think any post-2005 titanium driver, not a classic pear shape.

  • Tris 700–900
  • Texture 1024²
  • UVs crown / face / sole / skirt, 4 islands
Wood family · 02

Fairway wood head

Same family as the driver, scaled down: classic teardrop/pear head, shallower face, a small separate-looking sole plate rectangle for the metal-plate detail. Works for every "3W"/"5W" model since real fairway woods share this shape at any era.

  • Tris 500–700
  • Texture 1024²
  • UVs crown / face / sole plate, 3 islands
Crossover · 03

Hybrid head

Compact rounded wedge between an iron and a wood: shallow rounded sole like a mini fairway wood, but a taller, more upright face like an iron. Small enough to read as a "rescue" club, not a shrunk driver.

  • Tris 450–650
  • Texture 1024²
  • UVs crown / face / sole, 3 islands
Iron family · 04

Iron head

One representative head (roughly a 7-iron profile) stands in for the whole 4–9 set: thin blade-length body, flat sole, top-line bevel, a milled-looking cavity or flat back depending on skin. Thin enough to read as forged when the material calls for it.

  • Tris 500–700
  • Texture 1024²
  • UVs face / back-cavity / sole, 3 islands
Iron family · 05

Wedge head

The iron head, thickened: wider sole with a visible bounce radius, slightly more upright/square face, a touch more offset. One mesh covers PW, GW, SW and LW — loft is a stat, not a shape change.

  • Tris 550–750
  • Texture 1024²
  • UVs face / back / sole bounce, 3 islands
The exception

Putter heads — 9× unique

Not a shared mesh. See Section 06 for full geometry, material, and reference notes on all nine.

  • Tris 1,000–2,200 ea.
  • Texture 1024² ea.
  • Meshes 9 total

07

The putter collection · nine unique meshes, design freely

Every putter below is its own head mesh. The loaner is deliberately plain; the seven collectibles are the game's prestige objects and should look it in a cabinet.

Loaner Putter

loaner_putter · Loaner

"Dead and honest."

Plain straight blade, no offset, no alignment marks. Flat satin grey, bent shaft label peeling at the neck. Deliberately the least interesting object in the game.

Ref: any anonymous entry-level blade putter — no specific model, keep it generic.

Rustbelt Blade

rustbelt_blade · Common

"Clicky and honest. You hear every putt."

A no-frills straight blade with a centre neck bend, like a working-class Wilson 8802. Pitted orange-brown surface rust following the exact contour of the sweet spot — the wear pattern is the tell, not random speckle.

Material: aged/rusted mild steel. Dims: ~90mm face length, thin ~8mm blade depth.

Wilson 8802 blade – The Brassie 8802 review

Brass Relic

brass_relic · Uncommon

"Heavy and soft. Rolls the ball early."

A rounded flanged-blade head with a small tail extension behind a shaft set closer to centre — the classic mid-century "flange blade" shape. Solid cast brass, evenly darkened patina all over, deep green oxidation pooled in the flange corners and the cavity.

Material: solid brass, uneven all-over patina + verdigris in recesses. Dims: ~95mm face length, rounded toe and heel.

History of the Bulls Eye putter Bulls Eye putter – The Brassie

Midnight Mallet

midnight_mallet · Rare

"Muted thud. Hard to twist, easy to trust."

A rounded, high-MOI mallet body — oval from above, deep from face to back for perimeter weighting. Matte black finish with two crisp parallel white alignment lines running the full length of the top, plus a short centre tick. No wings; keep the outline smooth and rounded, not angular.

Material: matte black anodized/PVD, gloss-white inlay lines. Dims: ~120mm long, ~70mm wide, deep profile.

Spider Mallet 2.0 – TaylorMade Evolution of the Spider

Copper Classic

copper_classic · Epic

"Warm click, glassy release."

A precise heel-toe weighted blade — a hollowed centre section with distinct weight blocks left at the heel and toe, plumber's-neck hosel. This is the "classic Anser silhouette" done in solid warm copper: bright polished copper crown, satin copper face, no plating anywhere so it never shows wear-through.

Material: solid copper, polished top / satin face. Dims: ~100mm face length, visible milled heel and toe weight pads.

History of the PING Anser The Anser, most iconic putter?

Tourline Prototype

tourline_prototype · Epic

"Firm and stable, oddly quiet."

A raw-milled winged mallet: two small angular wing pontoons splayed off the back corners of a squared body, unfinished bead-blasted steel with no paint fill except one bold blue sightline down the centre. No brand mark, no model name — if anything, a small handwritten number on the sole.

Material: raw bead-blasted steel, single blue paint-fill line. Dims: ~115mm wide across the wings.

Spider Tour (winged prototype lineage)

Blackout Tour Issue

blackout_tour_issue · Legendary

"Dense and dead-center. Nothing rattles."

A precision tour blade, longer and heavier-looking than the loaner: deep milled face pattern, a full-length red sightline, a small removable weight port near the toe (a tour weight-kit detail). All-matte black finish, deliberately unmarked except a circular stamp hidden under where the grip sits.

Material: matte black PVD/DLC, red paint-fill sightline, one visible weight-port screw.

What "Circle T" means Tour-issue putter gallery

Scotty Cameron — The Grail

scotty_grail · Mythic

"That sound. Soft, solid, unmistakable."

The Newport silhouette: a compact, slightly flanged blade with a plumber's neck. Bright chrome/steel finish, deeply milled face, and the signature detail — three small cherry-red dots inlaid in a row on the cavity. Include a matching fitted headcover as a display prop; this is the one entry that should look expensive from across the room.

Material: polished 303 stainless look, milled face, three red-lacquer dot inlays. Dims: ~90mm face length, Newport proportions.

Grenade Prototype Driver

grenade_driver · Mythic (illegal)

Same driver head mesh as every other driver — the joke is entirely in the texture. Olive-drab crown with a laser-etched cross-hatch grenade-body pattern, a yellow-ringed "pin" decal near the hosel standing in for a safety pin, and a stencilled ordnance-style serial number on the sole. This is a direct nod to a real novelty club.

Auto-Putt 9000

auto_putt_9000 · Mythic (illegal)

The one item with nothing to reference in golf — go full gadget instead. A rounded, low mallet-sized chassis in gunmetal with a small teal-glass sensor dome on top (the "eye" that reads the green), a thin LED strip along the top-line that lights up teal when it locks a line, and a stubby, slightly oversized neck to hint at hidden servo motors. No alignment lines — it doesn't need your help lining up.
